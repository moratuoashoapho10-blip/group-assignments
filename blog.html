Five Days on Horseback: My Unforgettable Pony Trek Through the Maluti Mountains

When my guide handed me the reins of a sturdy Basotho pony at dawn in Semonkong, I had no idea that the next five days would completely reshape how I saw Southern Africa. The Basotho pony, a legendary breed born from the harsh highland climate is the original SUV of Lesotho, capable of navigating sheer cliff paths and icy river crossings with confident ease.

We wound our way through isolated mountain villages where children ran out to greet us with wide smiles and curious eyes. Each night we slept in rondavels circular thatched huts, listening to the wind howl across the mountain plateau. Our host families served papa and moroho, simple and nourishing food, always accompanied by steaming tea and generous laughter.

The highlight came on day three when we crested a ridge above 3,000 metres and the entire Lesotho highlands spread out before us a sea of golden grass interrupted only by ancient volcanic rock formations and the distant silver ribbon of the Senqu River. I understood then why travellers who discover Lesotho feel that the rest of the world has been keeping a tremendous secret.

Standing at the Edge of Maletsunyane Falls

At 192 metres, Maletsunyane Falls is one of Africa's most dramatic single-drop waterfalls. I hiked to the gorge in the early morning while mist still clung to the canyon walls. The thundering roar grows before you see anything and then suddenly, there it is. A white cascade plunging into a deep, mist-filled gorge surrounded by basalt cliffs. For daredevils, this is also where you can attempt one of the world's longest single-drop abseils. I chose to watch, photograph, and sip my flask of tea in stunned silence.

The Basotho Blanket: A Living Tradition Wrapped in History

Few items in African culture carry as much meaning as the Basotho blanket — seanamarena. Originally traded by European missionaries, these heavy wool blankets were adopted by King Moshoeshoe I and became the national symbol of identity and pride. Today, every blanket pattern tells a story: coronations, harvests, peace, and royal lineage. I visited a cooperative in Maseru where weavers demonstrated how each design is deliberate, cultural, and personal. Wearing a blanket isn't fashion — it's a declaration of belonging.

Skiing in Africa? Yes, at Afriski Mountain Resort

Most people laugh when I tell them I went skiing in Africa. Afriski Mountain Resort sits at 3,222 metres above sea level in the Maluti Mountains and offers the continent's best skiing outside of South Africa's own slopes. I went in July — Lesotho's winter — and found fresh snow, quad lifts, cosy mountain lodges, and an aprés-ski scene that felt wonderfully surreal. Whether you're a beginner or an intermediate skier, this is one of the most surprising experiences the continent has to offer.
